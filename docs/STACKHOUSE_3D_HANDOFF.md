# Stack House 3D Handoff

## Purpose

This document is the canonical starting point for a dedicated GPT-6 Astra task
working on the Stack House 3D experience. Read this document and
`docs/PROJECT_ARCHITECTURE.md` before inspecting or changing assets.

The first Astra turn must be a **read-only audit**. Do not modify Blender files,
GLBs, scene code, floor-plan data, colliders, or player controls until the user
has reviewed the audit and approved a staged plan.

## Product model

Stack House has two connected but distinct sides:

1. **Regular portfolio** — a cinematic, scroll-based exhibition at
   `/portfolio`.
2. **Stack House** — a first-person, browser-based 3D exploration at `/house`.

The root route (`/`) is the approved gate between them. The gate uses the real
house model and a cinematic Minneapolis skyline. The gate and regular portfolio
are approved work and are not a canvas for the 3D rebuild.

The house is the more demanding design problem. Its purpose is not merely to be
a realistic building. It is an explorable creative archive: rooms eventually
hold software, client work, games, music, history, experiments, and personal
storytelling.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- Do not redesign or rebuild the gate, regular portfolio, or their routing.
- Do not migrate the browser experience to Unreal Engine. An Unreal prototype
  may be proposed separately, but the production target remains Three.js/glTF.
- Do not overwrite existing GLBs or Blender sources. Create versioned assets.
- Do not treat a successful render as proof that walking, collisions, stairs,
  interactions, streaming, or mobile controls still work.
- Do not change `lib/interior-layout.ts` casually. It is authoritative input
  for visible shell generation, colliders, stairs, room placement, lighting,
  and vertical movement.
- Do not hand-edit generated `interior-*.glb` files without documenting how
  they will remain synchronized with their source layout.
- Preserve unrelated work and inspect current Git state before any action.
- Keep raw Blender/source assets outside `public/`. Only optimized runtime
  outputs belong in `public/models/`.
- Never present concept art as a prototype or gameplay capture.

## Important coupling

The interior is not an ordinary Blender-authored scene.

`lib/interior-layout.ts` defines room bounds, door openings, floor elevations,
stairs, room identities, and placement helpers. The build script converts that
layout into four GLBs. Collision is independently derived from the same layout.
The house route streams only the active floor and adjacent floors because the
stairs belong to the floor below and stairwells must remain visually continuous.

Any proposal to replace procedural shells with Blender-authored geometry must
explain how these stay synchronized:

- room and doorway geometry;
- floor origins and elevations;
- stair runs, half-landings, openings, and eye height;
- interior AABB colliders;
- room-based prop placement;
- room-based lighting;
- entry and exit spawn points;
- active-floor detection and adjacent-floor rendering.

A visual improvement that breaks this contract is a regression.

## Current performance decisions worth preserving

- Exterior scenery is hidden while indoors.
- Sky and stars are hidden while indoors.
- Only the active interior floor and adjacent floors render.
- Point lights use a fixed pool rather than mounting/unmounting arbitrary
  lights. Changing light count caused large shader-recompile stalls.
- The skyline is a single unlit plane rather than dozens of building meshes.
- Runtime GLBs are preloaded.
- The current house model is aligned to ground after measuring its bounding box.
- Raw/large source assets are intentionally excluded from deployment.

The audit should measure rather than assume: triangle counts, draw calls,
materials, textures, texture memory, node hierarchy, bounding boxes, origins,
normal/tangent quality, and runtime frame cost.

## Current spatial conventions

From the exterior collision documentation:

- X negative: player-left when facing the house from the street.
- X positive: driveway side.
- Z negative: street/player spawn.
- Z positive: backyard.
- Current measured exterior footprint before collision buffer:
  approximately X -4.735..6.172 and Z -4.471..4.539.
- Exterior model is currently rotated by PI and scaled uniformly by 8 in
  `components/house-model.tsx`.

Interior geometry is positioned around a large world-space X origin defined by
`X0` in `lib/interior-layout.ts`, keeping it separate from the yard. Inspect
the file for exact current values; do not copy coordinates from old notes.

## Known design and engineering concerns

Treat these as audit questions, not predetermined solutions:

- The exterior house has required substantial lighting and framing compensation.
  Determine whether geometry, materials, normals, scale, or art direction is
  responsible.
- Exterior and interior are separate systems. Determine whether the exterior can
  become a clean architectural shell that visually agrees with the generated
  interior without creating duplicate walls or occlusion artifacts.
- Generated interior geometry is intentionally a collision/proportion scaffold,
  not finished architectural artwork.
- The staircase needs modeled artwork that fits the documented socket while
  collision remains data-driven.
- Many rooms are deliberately unfurnished placeholders.
- Lighting is functional but should ultimately support room-specific narrative
  identity.
- The current runtime skyline may later be replaced with the approved cinematic
  skyline, but that is a separate scoped change.
- Desktop and mobile controls must continue to work.
- Existing static builds may emit non-fatal Three/GLTF `ProgressEvent is not
  defined` messages. Diagnose separately; do not conflate them with model
  quality.

## Desired art direction

Use the gate as the visual vocabulary, not as geometry to copy:

- black and deep navy;
- restrained Grey Key blue;
- sparse warm-gold practical light;
- cinematic nighttime Minneapolis atmosphere;
- mystery and depth rather than broad flat illumination;
- a real, inhabitable house rather than a generic game level;
- strong silhouettes and readable architecture;
- rooms that feel like distinct parts of one creative world.

The real Stack House model should remain recognizably the same place across the
gate and interactive experience.

## Blender source-of-truth strategy

Astra should propose, then document, a source strategy before producing assets.
Preferred principles:

- One clearly versioned Blender source collection for architectural art.
- Stable object and collection naming.
- Stable world origin, scale, and forward axis.
- Separate collections/exports for exterior shell, floors, staircase art,
  architectural trim, and optional furnishings.
- Runtime-safe PBR materials and packed/optimized textures.
- Predictable export paths and filenames.
- Non-destructive exports: never replace a working GLB until the candidate has
  passed browser verification.
- A manifest recording source file, export collection, version, dimensions,
  materials, textures, triangle count, and target runtime file.

The audit must decide whether Blender should own the finished visible shell
while TypeScript remains authoritative for gameplay/collision, or whether a
different boundary is safer.

## Recommended staged build after approval

1. **Exterior audit candidate**
   - Repair or rebuild only the exterior.
   - Export beside the current GLB.
   - Compare silhouette, materials, bounds, and performance in an isolated
     viewer before touching `/house`.
2. **One interior vertical slice**
   - Foyer, stair hall, and one adjacent room.
   - Prove alignment with the data-driven floor plan and collision.
3. **Staircase art**
   - Build against `docs/stair-socket.md`.
   - Keep movement/collision data-driven.
4. **Material and lighting pipeline**
   - Establish reusable architectural materials and lightmap/runtime strategy.
5. **Remaining architectural shell**
   - Finish floors without furnishing every room.
6. **One fully authored room**
   - Validate narrative, interaction, audio, and performance together.
7. **Remaining rooms and props**
   - Expand only after the vertical slice holds its frame budget.
8. **Final optimization and regression**
   - Desktop and mobile controls, entry/exit, all stairs, every floor, loading,
     route transitions, and production build.

## Definition of success

- The house looks materially better without losing its identity.
- The player can enter, exit, traverse every stair, and reach every intended
  room.
- Visible walls, doors, floors, stairs, and colliders agree.
- The gate and portfolio still route correctly.
- Runtime assets are optimized and versioned.
- No visual asset is promoted over the current production asset until measured
  and browser-tested.
- The workflow is reproducible by another agent from repository documentation.
